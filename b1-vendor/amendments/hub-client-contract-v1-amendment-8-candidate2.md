# Hub→client contract, Amendment 8 (candidate 2): the Hub terminates WebRTC; routes are channels on the client's one Hub peer connection

The base is the Hub→client contract of `frozen/current` (manifest final35), with Amendments 1 to 6, and HC Amendment 7 (accepted, manifest final36). They are unchanged except where A8-7 names a replacement. HC Amendment 7 touches no clause named here. Other contracts are cited by contract name and clause id only. **The route codec extract changes with this amendment:** revision 37 applies A8-7 to the codec's copies of the same clauses (the codec header: "The client contract owns this text"). The section "Route codec revision 37" gives the codec wording where it differs.

Author: the contract steward. Reviewer: the Amendment reviewer (Sol). Date: 2026-10-09. Authority:
- **a user decision, relayed by the orchestrator on 2026-10-09:** WebRTC moves out of Core and into the Hub. The Hub terminates WebRTC and bridges each route's data channel to a Core `Stream`. The trade-offs are accepted: a remote client reconnects when the Hub restarts, the Hub sees the plaintext route bytes, and there is one extra local hop. Core's side is Core Amendment 17;
- **the orchestrator's decisions of 2026-10-09, under the user's delegation:**
  - D1: route channels ride the client's one Hub peer connection. This settles the open item of section 13, "The `webrtc` binding's layout".
  - D2: route chunking is the Hub's, at `welcome.limits.max_chunk_bytes`, and `max_chunk_bytes` leaves `route_limits`.
  - Every route channel is chunked so that a large frame on one route cannot delay another route (A8-3).

**Supersedes candidate 1** (`84d577cb…d5b8`, commit `a748cc5`), **rejected** (verdict `2b245fa`). This candidate makes these 4 fixes:
1. **Fairness (finding 1).** A8-3 states a submission-order rule with a readiness condition. Payload that the association already holds is not reordered. The test holds the association's drain at a controlled point and asserts the submission order. It requires no unconditional receipt order.
2. **The chunk unit (finding 2).** `welcome.limits.max_chunk_bytes` bounds the chunk payload, without the `flags` byte, on every channel. So a message is at most `max_chunk_bytes + 1` bytes. TR-3's sizing sentence has a named replacement (A8-7 item 8), and the boundary test counts the `flags` byte separately.
3. **The failed-close drain (finding 3).** A8-4 makes draining mandatory only on a TS-8 healthy close. On a failed close, the Hub closes the channel without draining, and the route ends with its TS-9 reason. Two new ids cover a healthy Core close that drains and a stall close with an undrainable queue.
4. **Exact quotes (finding 4).** A8-7 quotes each replaced sentence or row exactly, and gives its new reading after it. The section "Route codec revision 37" gives the codec wording where it differs. A script copied every quotation from its cited file.

The other parts of candidate 1 are unchanged: A8-1, A8-2, A8-5 and A8-6, A8-4's queue parameter (moved from Core 9B, finding 5), D1, D2 and the trade-offs.

**Why.** Today the session worker terminates each route's own peer connection: TR-2, TB-W2, BP-1, BP-4, BP-7 and TB-W4, with Core DP-2, DP-3 and OU-3. Core Amendment 17 removes WebRTC from Core, so the route peer moves to the Hub. The Hub already terminates the client's control peer connection, and that connection is already mutually authenticated (PR-4 to PR-9). PR-8 already reads "no `hello`, no data and no route channel on that peer connection" before authentication. A route channel on that same connection inherits its authentication, so the per-route offer, answer, signature and fingerprint of TB-W2 and BP-4 are no longer needed.
