**Why.** PM-3: "The lock keeps the last `lock_history_max` entries per package for rollback." A2-29: a rollback with no older entry is `failed{detail.code: wrong_state, detail.reason: no_history}`. No clause says:
- whether the count includes the **current** lock;
- what a rollback does with the lock it replaces.

| A7-1 | **The lock history holds previous locks only, and rollback steps back through it.**<br>- **The current lock** is the package's lock record (PM-3). It is **not** counted in `lock_history_max`.<br>- **The history** is the list of previous lock records, newest first. When every entry was admitted under the current `lock_history_max`, it holds at most `lock_history_max` entries. Entries admitted under a larger limit before a live reduction may make it longer, until they age out by their own limit (below).<br>- **An install of a new version, or an update:** the current lock is pushed onto the history, and the new lock becomes current. **Each history entry keeps the limit L that was in force when it was pushed** (A2-52: an admitted lock entry keeps the capacity it was admitted under). After the push, **every entry's count of newer entries is taken over the complete post-push list, before any entry is dropped**, and then every entry whose count is at least its own L is dropped, all at once. With an unchanged limit, this is "keep the newest `lock_history_max`".<br>- **A rollback:** the newest history entry becomes the current lock, and it is removed from the history. **The lock it replaces is dropped**; a rollback never pushes onto the history, so there is no roll-forward through it. A later update is an explicit operation, as PM-3 requires.<br>- **A rollback with an empty history** is `failed{detail.code: wrong_state, detail.reason: no_history}` (A2-29), with effect `none`.<br>- **Example**, with `lock_history_max = 2`: install v1, then update to v2, v3 and v4 in turn. The current lock is v4, and the history is [v3, v2] (v1 was dropped). The first rollback makes v3 current, with history [v2]. The second makes v2 current, with history []. The third is `no_history`. So two previous versions are reachable.<br>- **A live change to `lock_history_max`** applies to new pushes only (A2-52). It never trims at the reload. Entries admitted under a larger limit age out by their own L.<br>- **Example of a reduction:** with `lock_history_max = 3`, the current lock is v4, and the history is [v3, v2, v1], each admitted with L = 3. The operator lowers the limit to 1. An update to v5 pushes v4 with L = 1, giving [v4, v3, v2, v1]. Then v1 has 3 newer entries, which is not less than its L = 3, so it is dropped: [v4, v3, v2]. The next update, to v6, pushes v5 with L = 1, giving [v5, v4, v3, v2]. v4 has 1 newer entry, which is not less than its L = 1, so it is dropped. v2 has 3 newer entries, so it is dropped. The result is [v5, v3]. Rollbacks then reach v5, then v3, then `no_history`. | `conf::pm_3_lock_history_bounded`, `conf::a7_1_current_lock_is_not_counted`, `conf::a7_1_rollback_drops_the_replaced_lock`, `conf::a7_1_rollback_past_the_history_is_no_history`, `conf::a7_1_reduction_keeps_entries_admitted_under_the_larger_limit`, `conf::a7_1_reduction_then_update_and_rollback` (asserts the history [v4, v3, v2] after the update to v5, and [v5, v3] after the update to v6) |
