### Accepted parts

- The diagnosis is sound and observed: an orphaned `tee` was still in the guarded group after the anchor's kill. On macOS, a
  `fork` that completes after `killpg` enumerated the group's members escapes that kill. Linux closes this window.
- The self-test now checks on every run that the blocked process exists in the guarded group before the kill, with
  `getpgid`. It does not rely on repeated runs.
- The A10 fixture in `slow_real_core.rs` starts its blocked `/bin/cat` before `ready`. It proves the end by EOF on its own
  FIFO, never by a probe of a pid.

#### J1 [MEDIUM] OPEN — The fix moves a guard defect into a precondition that no user can meet on panic or parent death

- Location: `process_guard.rs`, the new doc on `GroupGuard` ("Precondition of every user: kill only a quiet group"), and
  `anchor_process` (one `kill_process_group(group, KILL)`, then the anchor ends, because it is a member of that group).
- Evidence:
  - The anchor kills the group once. The kill also ends the anchor, so the anchor cannot repeat it.
  - The escaped child keeps the group id. The PR's own observation shows it: the orphaned `tee` was "still in the guarded
    group". A second group kill would end it, but no process is left to send one.
  - The precondition can hold only when the kill happens at a point that the test chooses. BUILD.md testing rule 10 needs the
    guard most on the other paths: a panic unwinds at any point, and the anchor's kill on parent death (EOF) comes at any
    point. Those paths can arrive while a fixture or a production worker is in a `fork`. Examples:
    - the worker's payload spawn;
    - a fixture pipeline;
    - the payload guard's helpers.
  - Existing users break the precondition by design. They fork every second until the kill:
    - `process_guard.rs`: `while :; do /bin/sleep 1; done` in the panic-before-ready self-test, and
      `(while :; do /bin/sleep 1; done) &` in `an_early_exit_keeps_the_group_owned_until_cleanup`;
    - botster-core `tests/common/mod.rs`: `WAIT_WHILE_THE_PARENT_LIVES`, which `slow_facade_worker.rs` and
      `slow_real_core.rs` use.
    Their groups are never quiet, so each of their kills can race a fork. The P5 reviewer found the same evidence
    independently.
  - The precondition binds every user in three packages: botster-core-sys, botster-core and botster-worker. This PR adapts
    only the self-test and the A10 fixture. No other user is audited against it, and no tool enforces it.
- Why: BUILD.md testing rule 10 ("Every real-process test owns its process group and cleans it up on every exit path,
  panics included"). The plan's real-process rule says that a guard owns the group, and it states no exception for timing.
  A precondition that callers must meet by order hides the defect. It does not fix it.
- Required:
  1. The guard ends every member of its group, including one that escaped the first kill, on every exit path. One possible
     way: the anchor stays a member, so the group id stays pinned. It ends every other member, by enumeration (on macOS
     `proc_listpids` with `PROC_PGRP_ONLY`) or by another means that does not kill the anchor. It repeats this until it is the
     only member, and then it exits. The kill loop needs a marked deadline. A member that cannot be ended is a reported failure.
  2. Add a deterministic regression test: a process that joins the group after the first kill still ends. No repetition
     campaign and no busy-spinning child.
  3. Delete the user precondition from the doc. The fixture reorderings may stay as good practice, but they do not close J1.
  4. If this cannot be done in this PR, ask the lead a QUESTION. Do not leave the precondition.
